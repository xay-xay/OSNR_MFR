Abstract: To address the challenge of achieving both high accuracy and low computational
complexityinjointmodulationformatrecognition(MFR)andopticalsignal-to-noiseratio(OSNR)
monitoring, a multi-feature fusion method based on a lightweight adaptive attention network
is proposed. A lightweight feature extraction scheme is first applied to the constant modulus
algorithm (CMA)-equalized signal to obtain multiple amplitude statistical and entropy features
for efficient signal representation. These features are then input into the proposed lightweight
adaptive multi-task squeeze-and-excitation deep neural network (LA-MT-SEDNN) integrated
with a hyperparameter tuning strategy based on Bayesian optimization (BO), enabling accurate
and low-complexity joint estimation. Experimental results in wavelength-division-multiplexed
(WDM) long-haul systems demonstrate strong accuracy and robustness under different OSNR
and channel conditions. Compared with a conventional multi-task neural network, the proposed
method achieves 100% MFR accuracy at minimum OSNR values reduced by 4 dB, 5 dB, 5 dB,
and2dBfor4QAM,8QAM,16QAM,and64QAM,respectively. ForOSNRmonitoring,itattains
0.065 dB MSE, 0.169 dB MAE, and 0.233 dB RMSE, with MAE and RMSE reduced by 63.9%
and 65.7% compared with MTL-CNN and adaptive MTL-DNN. Moreover, the multiplication
time complexity is reduced by approximately 98.9% and 95.2%, demonstrating significant
performance improvements with substantially lower computational complexity.

1. Introduction
The rapid expansion of data-intensive applications, including high-definition video, cloud com-
puting, and intelligent devices, has imposed increasingly stringent demands on network capacity
and flexibility, driving the evolution of optical networks toward dynamic and reconfigurable
architectures such as Elastic Optical Networks (EONs) [1–4]. By enabling fine-grained and
adaptive spectrum allocation, EONs significantly improve spectral efficiency and support flexible
adjustment of transmission parameters according to traffic conditions [5], establishing them as a
key foundation for next-generation intelligent optical networks [6,7]. However, ensuring stable
operation in such dynamic environments necessitates real-time monitoring of critical physical
layerparameters, particularlymodulationformatandOSNR.JointestimationofMFRandOSNR

is essential for reliable network scheduling and adaptive digital signal processing (DSP), yet
practical deployment faces two major challenges: OSNR directly affects signal equalization
quality and bit error rate performance [8], while inaccurate MFR may lead to improper DSP
configuration and subsequent signal recovery errors [9,10].
To address these challenges, coherent optical receivers should support simultaneous MFR
and OSNR estimation. Accurate OSNR monitoring provides physical-layer quality information
for optimized resource allocation, while real-time MFR enables adaptive DSP configuration,
reducing reliance on traditional handshake mechanisms and enhancing system autonomy [11]. In
recentyears,feature-basedMFRmethodshaveattractedattentionduetotheirlowcomplexityand
limited prior requirements, including approaches based on constellation analysis, histogram and
statistical features [12–14], as well as stokes space representations [15]. For OSNR monitoring,
existing techniques include statistical methods [16,17], Stokes space distribution-based schemes
[18,19], data-aided approaches [20], and amplitude distribution-based methods [21].
To enable simultaneous multi-parameter monitoring in EONs, various joint MFR and OSNR
schemes based on multi-task learning have been proposed. For example, MTL-DNN methods
improve monitoring performance through manually designed or adaptive task weights [22,23].
Other approaches extract Stokes vector distribution features to enhance accuracy [24], or adopt
image-based strategies such as CNNs with amplitude histograms and Radon transform-assisted
multi-task learning [25,26]. However, these methods often face limitations in accuracy or model
complexityundercomplexlinkconditions. Cascadedneuralnetworkarchitectureshavealsobeen
explored, but their OSNR estimation usually relies on prior knowledge of the modulation format.
Therefore, developing a joint monitoring scheme with both high accuracy and low complexity
remains essential for practical EON applications.
To overcome the difficulty of balancing high accuracy and low computational complexity in
joint MFR and OSNR monitoring, a multi-feature fusion for joint MFR and OSNR monitoring
based on LA-MT-SEDNN method is proposed. Multiple amplitude statistical features, including
the amplitude histogram (AH), amplitude mean square distance, amplitude dispersion standard
deviation and instantaneous velocity standard deviation together with entropy features are
extracted to achieve an efficient and compact representation of the signal characteristics. These
features are fed into the LA-MT-SEDNN for joint MFR and OSNR monitoring, where a channel
attentionmechanismadaptivelycapturesinter-featuredependenciesandhighlightsdiscriminative
information. Furthermore, an adaptive hyperparameter tuning strategy based on BO is integrated
into the network, this method enabling automated optimization of the model’s convergence
speed and generalization capability without manual intervention. Simulation and experimental
results verify the effectiveness of the proposed method. For 28 GBaud polarization division
multiplexing (PDM)-4QAM, 8QAM, 16QAM, 32QAM, and 64QAM signals, 100% MFR
accuracy is maintained across the entire OSNR range, while OSNR estimation achieves MSE,
MAE, and RMSE values as low as 0.088 dB, 0.226 dB, and 0.296 dB, respectively. The method
also demonstrates strong robustness under residual CD and nonlinear impairments. In WDM
long-haul transmission experiments, 100% MFR accuracy is achieved at minimum OSNR values
reduced by 4 dB, 5 dB, 5 dB, and 2 dB for 4QAM, 8QAM, 16QAM, and 64QAM, respectively.
Meanwhile, the OSNR monitoring MSE, MAE, and RMSE are reduced to 0.065 dB, 0.169 dB,
and0.233dB,respectively,withMAEandRMSEdecreasedby63.9%and65.7%comparedwith
MTL-CNN and adaptive MTL-DNN. Moreover, the multiplication time complexity is reduced
by approximately 98.9% and 95.2%, demonstrating that it achieves significant performance
improvement while maintaining exceptional computational efficiency.
2. Principle
The schematic diagram of the proposed scheme is shown in Fig. 1. At the receiver, the
optical signal is detected by a coherent receiver and processed by DSP modules, including IQ
===== PAGE 3 =====
Research Article Vol. 34, No. 8 / 20 Apr 2026 / Optics Express 14688
imbalance compensation, CD compensation, clock recovery, and CMA, to mitigate transmission
impairments. These operations are modulation-format independent. The equalized signal is
then delivered to the joint MFR and OSNR estimation module. In this module, a lightweight
feature extraction scheme combining amplitude statistical and entropy features is applied to the
CMA-equalized signal. Amplitude statistical features, including AH, amplitude mean square
distance, amplitude dispersion standard deviation, and instantaneous velocity standard deviation,
are extracted together with entropy features to provide a compact and informative representation.
The resulting features are fed into the proposed LA-MT-SEDNN for joint MFR and OSNR
monitoring. The LA-MT-SEDNN consists of a shared feature layer, an SE attention module, and
task-specific layers. The shared layer learns common representations for both tasks, while the SE
module enhances discriminative channels and suppresses redundant information by modeling
inter-channel dependencies. The task-specific layers are optimized separately for MFR and
OSNR, enabling parallel prediction. In addition, an adaptive hyperparameter tuning strategy is
incorporated to improve convergence efficiency and generalization performance without manual
adjustment.

2.2. LA-MT -SEDNN
Multi-Task Learning (MTL) is an inductive transfer strategy that improves generalization by
sharing feature representations across related tasks [27]. However, many existing MTL networks
methods enhance accuracy by increasing network depth and parameter scale, resulting in higher
computational complexity and limited deployment feasibility. To improve feature utilization
efficiency within a lightweight architecture, a lightweight attention-assisted multi-task network,
termedLA-MT-SEDNN,isproposedforcollaborativeMFRandOSNRmonitoring,asillustrated
in Fig. 5.
The model first takes a one-dimensional vector of 45 normalized features as input and extracts
low-level representations through a shared layer. This layer contains 64 fully connected neurons
with ELU activation, and Batch Normalization (BN) is applied to accelerate convergence and
enhance training stability.
After the shared layer, an SE module is introduced before the task-specific branches to
enhance feature selectivity by adaptively recalibrating channel-wise representations. Taking the
64-dimensional shared features as input, the module models inter-channel dependencies through
squeeze and excitation operations, strengthening task-relevant information while suppressing
redundancy. In the squeeze stage, a fully connected layer compresses the feature dimension

3. Simulation results and discussions
A 28 GBaud PDM-QAM simulation system is established using VPI Transmission Maker to
validate the effectiveness of the proposed multi-feature fusion for MFR and OSNR monitoring
based on LA-MT-SEDNN. The OSNR ranges are configured as 9∼20 dB for 4QAM, 10∼22 dB
for 8QAM, 15∼27 dB for 16QAM, 18∼30 dB for 32QAM, and 18∼30 dB for 64QAM with an
OSNR increment of 1 dB. For each OSNR value of each modulation format, 100 sets of data
samples are generated, with each sample containing a one-dimensional vector feature composed
of 16,384 symbols. Ultimately, 1,200 groups of samples were obtained for 4QAM, and 1,300
groups each for 8QAM, 16QAM, 32QAM, and 64QAM. The dataset is randomly divided into
70% for training, 20% for testing, and 10% for validation. The number of training epochs is set
to 300, and an early stopping strategy is employed during the training process. Specifically, early
stopping is triggered based on the validation loss of the OSNR estimation task. If the validation
loss does not improve for 200 consecutive epochs, the training process is terminated early.
Figure 6 illustrates the OSNR monitoring results for 4QAM, 8QAM, 16QAM, 32QAM, and
64QAM in a back-to-back (BTB) transmission link. The recognition accuracy (RA) of the five
modulation formats remain at 100% throughout the entire OSNR range. In addition, the OSNR
monitoring average MAEs for 4QAM, 8QAM, 16QAM, 32QAM, and 64QAM are as low as
0.157 dB, 0.188 dB, 0.194 dB, 0.271 dB, and 0.313 dB, respectively, demonstrating excellent
OSNRmonitoringperformance. Overall,theproposedmethoddemonstratesaccuratemodulation
recognition and OSNR monitoring.

4. Experiment setups and results and discussions
Figure11illustratesthearchitectureofthecoherentWDMopticaltransmissionsystem,including
the transmitter configuration and the receiver-side digital signal processing (DSP). At the
transmitter, quadrature amplitude modulation (QAM) signals are generated through a DSP
chain comprising pseudo-random binary sequence (PRBS) generation, QAM mapping, and
up-sampling. The electrical signals are output by an arbitrary waveform generator (AWG),
amplified by an electrical amplifier (EA), and used to modulate two orthogonally polarized
optical carriers emitted from a laser diode (LD). The optical carrier is split by a polarization
beam splitter (PBS), independently modulated by in-phase/quadrature (I/Q) modulators, and
recombined by a polarization beam combiner (PBC) to form a single transmission channel.
Nine wavelength channels corresponding to International Telecommunication Union Telecom-
munication Standardization Sector (ITU-T) channels C37 to C29, centered at 1548.518 nm,
1549.320 nm, 1550.120 nm, 1550.918 nm, 1551.721 nm, 1552.524 nm, 1553.329 nm, 1554.134
nm, and 1554.940 nm, are multiplexed by a wavelength division multiplexer (WDM Mux)
and amplified by an erbium-doped fiber amplifier (EDFA) before transmission over three 80
km spans of standard single-mode fiber (SSMF), with EDFAs deployed between spans. The
optical fiber parameters include an attenuation coefficient of 0.2 dB/km, a CD coefficient of 17
ps/nm·km, and a nonlinear coefficient of 1.3km−1·W−1. At the receiver, the target channel is

5. Conclusion
A multi-feature fusion method for joint MFR and OSNR monitoring using a lightweight adaptive
attention network is proposed in the paper. Compact and informative features are extracted from
CMA-equalized signals and processed by the proposed LA-MT-SEDNN with BO for accurate
low-complexity estimation. Experiments in WDM systems demonstrate strong robustness across
OSNR levels and channel counts. The method achieves 100% MFR accuracy with minimum
OSNR requirements reduced by 4 dB, 5 dB, 5 dB, and 2 dB for 4QAM, 8QAM, 16QAM, and
64QAM, respectively. OSNR monitoring achieves an MSE of 0.065 dB, an MAE of 0.169
dB, and an RMSE of 0.233 dB. Compared with MTL-CNN and adaptive MTL-DNN, MAE
and RMSE are reduced by 63.9% and 65.7%, while multiplication complexity is reduced by
approximately 98.9% and 95.2%, demonstrating substantial performance gains with minimal
computational cost.
